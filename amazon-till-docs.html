1. What Your Till Does

Your system is an Electronic Point of Sale (EPOS) workstation designed to keep your counter and service running smoothly:

  • It adds up food orders, hot drinks, bakery items, and custom modifiers so staff never have to do mental arithmetic under pressure.
  • It links seamlessly with card readers to take contactless cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, and Chip & PIN payments in seconds.
  • It records cash payments and works out the exact change to hand back to the customer.
  • It prints clean paper receipts with your North London cafe address, phone number, VAT registration, and the British Pound (£) symbol.
  • It supports table tabs, takeaway orders, and staff tip / gratuity pooling.
  • It totals up all your daily takings automatically, saving you evening paperwork and cash float stress.

2. The Parts of Your Till

Your countertop setup consists of four core elements:

  • The Touchscreen Terminal: SumUp Point of Sale Lite 13-inch Full HD display on an ergonomic stand facing your barista or counter staff.
  • The Card Reader (PDQ): SumUp Solo Contactless & Chip Card Reader paired wirelessly with the register.
  • The Receipt & Order Printers: Front checkout thermal receipt printer plus a secondary kitchen ticket printer for automatic order routing.
  • The Cash Drawer: First-party SumUp Cash Drawer (£69.00 ex VAT / £82.80 incl. VAT) connected via an RJ11 cable to the back of the checkout receipt printer. It triggers open automatically whenever you record a cash payment.

3. Turning It On & Internet

Every morning:

  1. Press and hold the power button on the back or right edge for 2 seconds. The screen will illuminate and boot up.
  2. Confirm that the Wi-Fi icon in the top right shows strong signal (or the Ethernet symbol shows connected).
  3. Open the POS application on the home screen.
  4. Enter your morning staff PIN code to unlock the register.

4. Taking a Card Payment — Step by Step

  1. Select the drinks or food items (e.g. Flat White, Eggs Florentine, Avocado Toast) or tap custom amount.
  2. Tap any required modifiers (e.g. Oat Milk, Extra Shot, Sourdough).
  3. Tap the blue or green Charge / Pay button on the screen.
  4. Select Card as the payment method. The amount transmits to the card reader (or staff types the amount on a standalone terminal).
  5. Invite the customer to tap their contactless card/phone or insert their chip card.
  6. Wait for the green tick and confirmation chime on the card screen.
  7. Ask if the customer would like a printed paper receipt.
  8. Tap Finish Sale for the next customer.

5. Taking a Cash Payment

  1. Select the items as usual and tap Charge / Pay.
  2. Tap Cash.
  3. Tap the exact amount handed to you (e.g. £20 note).
  4. The screen displays the exact change due to the penny, and the cash drawer clicks open automatically.
  5. Place the cash in the drawer, count the change into the customer's hand, and close the drawer firmly.

6. Modifiers, Add-ons & Split Bills

Cafes and brunch spots frequently handle custom drink preparations and customers who want to split the bill:

  • Barista Drink Modifiers: Tap any coffee to add milk alternatives (oat, almond, coconut), extra espresso shots, decaf beans, or flavoured syrups. These are priced automatically.
  • Gratuities / Tips: When customers wish to leave a tip on card, select the gratuity prompt on the card reader before the final charge.
  • Split Bills: For brunch tables sharing dishes, tap Split Bill to divide the total evenly across multiple cards, or charge specific items separately.

7. Takeaway vs Eat-in Orders

Your till distinguishes between eat-in dining and takeaway counter service:

  • Eat-in Service: Assign a table number so front-of-house staff know where to deliver brunch plates and coffee.
  • Takeaway Orders: Flag orders as takeaway to ensure correct VAT attribution (standard 20% on hot food and eat-in vs zero-rated cold takeaway food items where applicable).

8. Giving a Refund

If a client needs a refund or an accidental double-charge occurred:

  1. Tap the menu button (three horizontal lines) and select Transactions / Sales History.
  2. Locate the transaction by time, amount, or last 4 digits of the card.
  3. Tap Issue Refund.
  4. Select the specific item or enter a custom refund amount.
  5. Select refund to original card or cash, and confirm. The refund receipt will print automatically.

9. Daily Takings & Z-Reports (Cashing Up)

At the close of trading:

  1. Tap Reports → Close Register (commonly called the End-of-Day or Z-Report).
  2. Count the physical cash in the drawer and enter the total. The till will tell you if your float is exact, over, or short.
  3. Tap Print Daily Summary. A comprehensive paper slip will print listing total card sales, cash takings, VAT breakdowns, and shift totals.
  4. File the report with your daily receipts for your accountant.

10. Receipt Paper Replacement

When a red or pink stripe appears on the edge of receipts, your paper roll is nearly empty:

  1. Pull the top lever on the printer to open the clamshell lid.
  2. Drop in a fresh roll of thermal paper (shiny side facing toward the print head, paper feeding from the bottom of the roll).
  3. Pull 2 inches of paper past the tear-off blade and snap the lid shut firmly until it clicks.
  4. Tear off the excess paper downwards across the serrated blade.

11. Troubleshooting Common Glitches

Quick Fixes for Counter Glitches

1. The till screen is frozen: Hold down the power button on the back for 10 seconds until the screen shuts off. Wait 5 seconds, then turn it back on. No sales data will be lost.

2. Card reader says "Connection Error": Check that Bluetooth or Wi-Fi is toggled ON in the till settings. If on standalone terminal, ensure the card machine display shows ready with green battery bars.

3. Cash drawer does not click open: Check the telephone-style RJ11 cable behind the printer is firmly clicked into the socket marked "DK" or "Drawer".

13. Cafe Care & Liquid / Steam Protection

Protecting Countertop Electronics in a Busy Cafe

Cafes and coffee counters are demanding environments with steam wands, boiling water, espresso grinds, milk jugs, and sticky syrups. Keep the till terminal at least 1 metre away from the espresso machine steam wand, sink splash zones, and syrup dispensers. Clean the glass touchscreen only with a soft microfibre cloth dampened lightly with water or screen-safe electronic wipes — never spray antibacterial kitchen cleaners or water directly onto the screen or card reader.

15. Glossary of Terms

  • EPOS: Electronic Point of Sale — the computerised till and register.
  • PDQ: Process Data Quickly — industry term for the customer card payment terminal.
  • Modifier: An option attached to a menu item (e.g. oat milk, extra shot, decaf, sourdough toast) that automatically adjusts the price and kitchen ticket.
  • ESC/POS: Standard printing command language used by receipt printers.
  • Code Page 858: Printer character memory setting that allows the British Pound (£) symbol to print without errors.
  • Solenoid: The magnetic coil inside the cash drawer that pulls the latch to pop it open when triggered.
  • Z-Report: The end-of-day daily financial summary that closes out the trading day.
