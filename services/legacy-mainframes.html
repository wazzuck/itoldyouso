Archaic & Legacy Mainframe FAQs

Can you really work on 1970s and 1980s mainframes, minicomputers, and archaic software in London?

Yes. Backed by over 20 years of systems engineering experience—including mission-critical financial infrastructure in the City of London—we work directly on 1960s to 1990s architectures including IBM System/360, System/370, and AS/400, ICL systems, DEC PDP-11 and VAX/VMS, Tandem NonStop, HP 3000, CP/M, MS-DOS, OS/2, and heritage COBOL, FORTRAN, RPG, and PL/I codebases.

Can you extract data from EBCDIC tapes, 8-inch or 5.25-inch floppies, and legacy COBOL or VSAM files?

Yes. We recover raw bitstreams from 9-track magnetic tapes, Quarter-Inch Cartridges (QIC), Digital Audio Tape (DAT), 8-inch and 5.25-inch floppy disks, and ageing SCSI drives, then decode Extended Binary Coded Decimal Interchange Code (EBCDIC) and packed-decimal (COMP-3) COBOL, VSAM, or dBASE records cleanly into modern UTF-8 CSV and SQL databases.

Can you keep our irreplaceable legacy system running without relying on failing 40-year-old hardware?

Yes. Using cycle-accurate emulators such as Hercules for IBM mainframes, SIMH for DEC PDP-11 and VAX systems, and QEMU or 86Box for DOS, OS/2, and early Windows platforms, we lift your untouched operating system and applications onto resilient modern Linux hosts with snapshot backups and solid-state storage.

Can you connect an ancient green-screen terminal system to modern websites, accounts, or Point of Sale (POS) software?

Yes. We build clean bridges over 3270, 5250, VT100, and RS-232 serial interfaces or batch exports, wrapping trusted legacy logic in modern Application Programming Interfaces (APIs) so newer web portals, accounting tools, and Point of Sale (POS) systems talk safely to your core records.
