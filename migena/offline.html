    Administration — Staff Accounts

    Admin Guide — Adding and Removing Staff

    For the owner and the manager: how to add a staff member, change what they are allowed to do, reset a forgotten Personal Identification Number (PIN), and switch off a leaver without losing a single day's figures.

    Who Should Do This Work

    Only the owner or the manager. Do it at a quiet time, on a computer or tablet, in the SumUp web dashboard at sumup.me, where every setting sits on one screen and the register can carry on taking orders while you work. Counter staff do not need this guide.

    User Names and Passwords Are on a Separate Page

    The account logins are deliberately not written in this guide. They live on a separate page: open the offline copy of this documentation and scroll to User Passwords, or ask your engineer for the current list.

    1. Your Team at a Glance

    The installation created four profiles with individual PINs. Refunds and discounts are restricted to the manager profile, and every other profile can sell only.

    Profile Can do Cannot do
    Owner account
    Held by the account holder     		Everything: staff, plan, payouts, reports and settings —
    Manager
    Held by the manager on duty     		Sell, refund, discount, run the end-of-day close, read reports Change the SumUp account or the bank account payouts go to
    Counter staff (three profiles)
    One PIN each, never shared     		Sell, split bills, park orders, reprint receipts Refunds, discounts, reports, staff changes

    2. Add a Staff Member

    1. Sign in to sumup.me as the owner. The verification code arrives on the owner's mobile phone, so have it to hand.
    2. Open the team / employee management area. Depending on the version it is labelled Employees, Team or Staff.
    3. Choose Add employee, or the plus button.
    4. Type the staff member's name. An e-mail address or position title is optional: add it only if you want it in the reports.
    5. Choose the permission level: staff for counter work, manager for refunds, discounts, reports and the close.
    6. Set the PIN: open the employee, choose PIN, switch on PIN access, then either let SumUp generate a code or type the one you have chosen.
    7. Save, then hand the PIN over privately — in person or by telephone, never in a group chat and never on a note stuck to the counter.
    8. Test it on the register: sign out, sign in with the new PIN, ring one cheap item, and confirm it appears under their name in the day's figures.

    Menu Names Vary Between SumUp Versions

    If a screen does not match the steps above, stop and ring 020 4520 9114 rather than guessing. A wrong toggle in this area can open refunds to everyone on the counter.

    3. Change What Someone Can Do

    Use this to promote a trusted counter worker to manager, or to take refund power away again after a role change.

    1. Open the person in the Employees screen.
    2. Change their permission level, or tick the individual permissions your version shows: refunds, discounts, sales history, reports.
    3. Save, then test it: sign in as them and confirm a restricted action is refused.

    A Manager PIN Is a Refund PIN

    Give the manager level only to someone you trust with the money, and review the list after every promotion, every leaver, and any incident you would rather not repeat.

    4. Reset a Forgotten PIN

    1. Open the person in the Employees screen.
    2. Choose PIN and generate a new code, or type a new one.
    3. Tell them privately. The old code stops working at once.
    4. Never write the new code on the till, on any printed sheet, or in a photo on a phone.

    Keep One Spare, Sealed

    Keep a single spare manager PIN in a sealed envelope for the day someone forgets theirs mid-rush, and change it as soon as it has been used.

    5. Remove a Staff Member

    SumUp does not delete employee profiles, and that is a good thing: switching a profile off keeps every past sale attached to the right name, so the figures for earlier days stay correct.

    1. Do it on the day they leave, before their next shift could start.
    2. Open the person in the Employees screen.
    3. Switch their profile off (deactivate) — in current versions this is the toggle at the top of the screen.
    4. Check their PIN no longer unlocks the register.
    5. If they ever come back, switch the profile on again and issue them a fresh PIN.

    Shared PIN Suspected? Reset, Do Not Deactivate

    If you only suspect that a PIN is being lent out, change the PIN instead: it takes seconds, keeps the sales history intact, and the person keeps working. Deactivation is for leavers.

    6. Security Rules That Keep the Figures Honest

    • One PIN per person: never lend it, never share it, never let two people work under one profile.
    • Give every new starter a fresh PIN on day one, and never recycle a leaver's code.
    • Only the owner knows the account password, and only the owner's phone receives the two-factor authentication (2FA) codes.
    • Refunds and discounts stay manager-only.
    • Read each staff member's sales total once a week: a name with far more sales than their shifts suggest usually means a shared PIN.

    7. When to Ring the Engineer

    Ring 020 4520 9114 before changing anything if a screen does not match this guide, if a PIN or a profile has vanished, or if anything to do with money looks wrong. Five minutes on the telephone beats a wrong toggle that opens refunds to the whole counter.

    Management — Accounts & Records

    Management Guide — Getting Your Accounts Organised

    For the owner: the accounts that sit behind the till, who holds each one, and the routines that keep the numbers straight. It costs about fifteen minutes a week and half an hour a month, and it is what turns a busy till into books your accountant can read without telephoning you.

    Five Accounts, One Rule

    Everything below comes back to one rule: each account has exactly one holder, and no password or verification code is ever written down in this portal. When everybody knows which key opens which door, month-end takes half an hour instead of a weekend.

    1. The Five Accounts to Keep Organised

    1. The SumUp merchant account (sumup.me): sales, staff profiles, the catalogue, the plan and every setting live here. Held by the owner.
    2. The bank account payouts go to: the business current account that SumUp pays into.
    3. The till profiles: owner, manager and three counter staff, each with their own PIN. See the Admin Guide.
    4. The daily paperwork: Z-reports, refund receipts and payout summaries, filed together.
    5. This portal: these guides, the printable cheat sheet once it unlocks, and the support number.

    2. Who Holds What

    Account or code Who holds it, and where it lives If it is lost or forgotten

    Never Write Down a Secret

    Do not store a password or a verification code in this document, in an e-mail, or in a shared photograph. Staff names and sales figures are personal data, so the United Kingdom General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) applies: keep them minimal, and keep them private.

    3. The Plan and the Fees

    • POS Plus is the paid plan that carries kitchen order synchronisation, employee PINs with permissions, till management and the cashbook, and the fuller reports. It costs about £39 a month excluding Value Added Tax (VAT) as published by SumUp; check SumUp profile → Plans and fees for the price actually on your account.
    • Payouts are sent on business days and normally reach the bank within one to two working days. How often they are sent — daily, weekly or monthly — is set in your SumUp payout settings, so check once that it matches how you like to run the money.
    • Card fees come out of the payout, so the amount landing in the bank is your takings minus fees. Do not expect the bank line to equal the Z-report total: match the Z-report to the payout report in SumUp instead, and treat the difference as fees.

    4. The Daily Rhythm

    1. Open using the opening steps in Till Procedures.
    2. Trade: every sale rung under the person who took it, so the week's figures mean something.
    3. Close using the close-down steps in Till Procedures: count, print the Z-report, take the takings out, leave the float.
    4. Clip that day's refund receipts to the Z-report and initial them.

    5. Every Week (about 15 Minutes)

    • Download the week's sales export from Reports as a CSV file.
    • Read back every refund issued this week; each one should have a story you can tell.
    • Check each staff member's total against the rota, and ask about any name that does not fit.
    • Confirm every expected payout reached the bank.
    • Check receipt rolls, till paper, and that the kitchen Terminal is charged and syncing.

    6. Every Month (about 30 Minutes)

    • Reconcile: add up the Z-reports, compare them with SumUp's payout reports, then with the bank statement, and note the difference as card fees.
    • Hand the Z-reports, the payout summary and the VAT figures to the bookkeeper.
    • Review the staff list: remove leavers and confirm roles, per the Admin Guide.
    • Review the plan under SumUp profile → Plans and fees: still needed, and any price change?
    • Back up the catalogue from Items → Export, and save the CSV file with that month's folder.
    • Apply app updates before a quiet morning, then re-brief the team for five minutes.

    Till Procedures

    Who this is for

    1. Every member of front-of-house staff.
    2. The manager, who also has the refund and close-down steps.

    Words you will see on the screen

    1. POS — Point of Sale. The till app on the Chromebook.
    2. Cart — the list of items for the table you are serving now.
    3. Park order — save an order to come back to later. Nothing is paid.
    4. Order note — a free-text message for the kitchen, such as an allergy.
    5. PIN — personal identification number, your four-digit login.
    6. Solo — the card machine that takes the customer's payment.

    Before you start

    1. The Chromebook is on and the POS app is open.
    2. The card machine (Solo) shows a green or charged screen.
    3. Your PIN is known to you only. Never share it.

    1. Opening the till (first person in, about 10 minutes)

    1. Switch on the Chromebook and open the POS app.
    2. Log in with your PIN.
    3. Check the top bar shows the shop name and no warning banner.
    4. Check the printer has paper. If not, load a roll (section 13).
    5. Count the cash float into the till tray. Write the total on the cash sheet and have a second person check it.
    6. Check the kitchen device is showing the open order list.
    If a warning banner appears: note its words, then go to section 14.

    2. Logging in and out

    1. Tap your name or the login prompt.
    2. Enter your PIN.
    3. When your shift ends, log out. The account icon (bottom left) is where to look; the exact Log out label is not yet confirmed (section 15).
    Never leave the till logged in and unattended.

    3. Taking a table order

    1. Tap New order on the Open orders screen (the bell icon on the left bar), or tap the cart icon to start a new cart.
    2. Choose the menu category along the top, such as 03 Brunch & Beyond. Tap All to see everything.
    3. Tap an item to add it to the cart.
    4. Check the cart on the right. Each line shows the quantity and price.
    5. Repeat for every item the table has ordered.
    6. Tap Park order if the table is still deciding. The button that sends the order to the kitchen is not yet confirmed (section 15).
    7. Confirm the order appears on the kitchen display (section 9).

    4. Modifiers (choices such as milk or bread)

    Modifiers are options attached to an item. The till asks for them after you tap the item.

    1. Tap the item.
    2. Choices are expected to appear, such as Milk (oat, almond, soya, coconut) or Bread. The exact prompt is not yet confirmed on the device (section 15); stop and ask the manager if it differs.
    3. Tap the customer's choice and confirm it.
    4. Check the modifier is written under the item name in the cart.

    Common modifier groups in this shop

    1. Drinks: milk choice, extra shot, decaf, syrups.
    2. Food: bread, egg style, eggs benedict choice, omelette fillings, pasta type, jacket potato filling.

    5. Allergies and special requests

    Rule 1: every allergy goes in the order note. A modifier is for choices on the menu; the order note is for anything else, including allergies.

    Rule 2: say it aloud to the kitchen. The order note is a written message, so a second check by voice is still needed.

    How to add an order note

    1. Tap the three dots at the top right of the cart.
    2. Tap Add order note.
    3. Type the allergy in plain words, for example: NUT ALLERGY — no nuts, no nut oil, check the sauce.
    4. Tap Save. The note is saved with the order.
    5. Send the order (section 15 notes the button is not yet confirmed), then tell the kitchen in person.
    What not to do: never write only "allergy" with no detail, and never remove the note by accident. If the customer changes their mind, edit the note rather than deleting it.

    6. Changing or cancelling items

    1. Clear the whole cart: tap the three dots, then Clear cart. Use this only for a cart that has not been sent to the kitchen.
    2. Change one item: remove it, then add the correct one.
    3. Item already sent to the kitchen: tell the manager. Do not cancel it on the till without them.

    7. Discounts and manual prices (manager only)

    1. Tap the three dots, then Add discount, or Manual price entry.
    2. Enter the amount or percentage, and the reason.
    3. The manager enters their PIN to confirm.

    8. Taking payment

    8.1 Card (Solo)

    1. Tap Card at the bottom right of the cart.
    2. Choose Card Reader. The payment amount appears on the Solo.
