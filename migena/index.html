1. What Your Till Does

Your system is an Electronic Point of Sale (EPOS) workstation designed to keep your reception desk running smoothly:

  • It adds up client services and retail products so you never have to do mental arithmetic under pressure.
  • It links seamlessly with card readers to take contactless cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, and Chip & PIN payments.
  • It records cash payments and works out the exact change to hand back to the customer.
  • It prints clean paper receipts with your North London salon address, phone number, VAT registration, and the British Pound (£) symbol.
  • It tracks individual therapist sales so commission calculations at the end of the week or month take seconds instead of hours.
  • It totals up all your daily takings automatically, saving you evening paperwork.

2. The Parts of Your Till

Your countertop setup consists of four core elements:

  • The Touchscreen Terminal: The main screen facing your receptionist or stylist. You touch tiles to choose treatments, enter prices, and complete sales.
  • The Card Reader (PDQ): Your card machine (Dojo, Square, Zettle, or bank terminal) where clients tap or insert their cards.
  • The Thermal Receipt Printer: Prints on 58mm or 80mm heat-sensitive paper without ink or ribbons.
  • The Cash Drawer: Connected via an RJ11/RJ12 cable. It triggers open automatically whenever you record a cash payment.

3. Turning It On & Internet

Every morning:

  1. Press and hold the power button on the back or right edge for 2 seconds. The screen will illuminate and boot up.
  2. Confirm that the Wi-Fi icon in the top right shows strong signal (or the Ethernet symbol shows connected).
  3. Open the POS application on the home screen.
  4. Enter your morning staff PIN code to unlock the register.

4. Taking a Card Payment — Step by Step

  1. Select the treatments (e.g. Cut & Blow Dry, Gel Manicure, Facial) or tap custom amount.
  2. Tap the blue or green Charge / Pay button on the screen.
  3. Select Card as the payment method. The amount transmits to the card reader (or staff types the amount on a standalone terminal).
  4. Invite the client to tap their contactless card/phone or insert their chip card.
  5. Wait for the green tick and confirmation chime on the card screen.
  6. Ask if the client would like a printed paper receipt.
  7. Tap Finish Sale for the next client.

5. Taking a Cash Payment

  1. Select the services or items as usual and tap Charge / Pay.
  2. Tap Cash.
  3. Tap the exact amount handed to you (e.g. £50 note).
  4. The screen displays the exact change due to the penny, and the cash drawer clicks open automatically.
  5. Place the cash in the drawer, count the change into the client's hand, and close the drawer firmly.

6. Split Services, Tips & Retail Product Sales

Salons often combine multiple therapists on one bill (e.g. colourist plus stylist) or combine hair services with shampoo bottles:

  • Therapist Attribution: Before finishing the sale, tap the service item to assign the stylist who performed it. This ensures fair commission reporting.
  • Gratuities / Tips: When the client wishes to leave a tip on card, select the gratuity prompt on the card reader before the final charge.
  • Retail Skincare & Haircare: Select from the Retail category. Retail items are flagged at standard 20% VAT, while medical or exempt aesthetic treatments are handled according to your tax classification.

7. Booking Deposits & Vouchers

If a client paid a £20 deposit when booking their appointment over the phone or online:

  • Ring up the full treatment price on the till.
  • Tap Apply Deposit / Discount and enter £20.
  • The till calculates the remaining balance to charge on card or cash.

8. Giving a Refund

If a client needs a refund or an accidental double-charge occurred:

  1. Tap the menu button (three horizontal lines) and select Transactions / Sales History.
  2. Locate the transaction by time, amount, or last 4 digits of the card.
  3. Tap Issue Refund.
  4. Select the specific item or enter a custom refund amount.
  5. Select refund to original card or cash, and confirm. The refund receipt will print automatically.

9. Daily Takings & Z-Reports (Cashing Up)

At the close of trading:

  1. Tap Reports → Close Register (commonly called the End-of-Day or Z-Report).
  2. Count the physical cash in the drawer and enter the total. The till will tell you if your float is exact, over, or short.
  3. Tap Print Daily Summary. A comprehensive paper slip will print listing total card sales, cash takings, VAT breakdowns, and therapist totals.
  4. File the report with your daily receipts for your accountant.

10. Receipt Paper Replacement

When a red or pink stripe appears on the edge of receipts, your paper roll is nearly empty:

  1. Pull the top lever on the printer to open the clamshell lid.
  2. Drop in a fresh roll of thermal paper (shiny side facing toward the print head, paper feeding from the bottom of the roll).
  3. Pull 2 inches of paper past the tear-off blade and snap the lid shut firmly until it clicks.
  4. Tear off the excess paper downwards across the serrated blade.

11. Troubleshooting Common Glitches

Quick Fixes for Reception Glitches

1. The till screen is frozen: Hold down the power button on the back for 10 seconds until the screen shuts off. Wait 5 seconds, then turn it back on. No sales data will be lost.

2. Card reader says "Connection Error": Check that Bluetooth or Wi-Fi is toggled ON in the till settings. If on standalone terminal, ensure the card machine display shows ready with green battery bars.

3. Cash drawer does not click open: Check the telephone-style RJ11 cable behind the printer is firmly clicked into the socket marked "DK" or "Drawer".

13. Salon Care & Chemical Protection

Protecting Countertop Electronics in a Beauty Salon

Beauty salons use chemicals that can degrade plastic casings and touchscreens over time. Keep hair bleach, tinting bowls, acetone nail polish remover, and aerosol hairsprays at least 2 metres away from the till. Clean the glass screen only with a soft microfibre cloth dampened lightly with water or screen-safe alcohol wipes — never spray liquids directly onto the touchscreen.

15. Glossary of Terms

  • EPOS: Electronic Point of Sale — the computerised till and register.
  • PDQ: Process Data Quickly — industry term for the customer card payment terminal.
  • ESC/POS: Standard printing command language used by receipt printers.
  • Code Page 858: Printer character memory setting that allows the British Pound (£) symbol to print without errors.
  • Solenoid: The magnetic coil inside the cash drawer that pulls the latch to pop it open when triggered.
  • Z-Report: The end-of-day daily financial summary that closes out the trading day.
